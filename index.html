Why do high-volume small turned parts run on gang-type CNC lathes?

Gang-type lathes mount tools directly on the cross slide, so there is no turret indexing between operations. On small parts produced in batches of 10,000 pieces or more, the seconds saved every cycle compound into much higher throughput and a lower per-piece price. Our turning shop runs 50 CNC lathes and 70 gang-type sliding-head machines, producing up to 5 million pieces per month. Read the full guide: Gang-type CNC lathe mass production.

Which aluminum alloys are best for CNC machined parts?

6061-T6 for general strength, corrosion resistance and weldability; 7075-T6 where strength-to-weight matters (robotics, aerospace-adjacent structures); 5083 for marine and welded fabrications. All three are stocked in plate and bar in our Shenzhen workshop. Details: Aluminum CNC machining parts.

What is 3+2 axis CNC machining and when does it beat full 5-axis?

In a 3+2 configuration the two rotary axes lock at fixed angles and cutting happens with three linear axes. For prismatic parts machined on a few faces — brackets, housings, fixtures — 3+2 delivers 5-axis-class accuracy at lower cycle cost. 30 of our 100 five-axis machining centers run 3+2 configurations. When is Swiss-type turning the right choice?

For parts with a length-to-diameter ratio above roughly 3:1 and diameters under 20 mm — pins, bushings, connector and medical components — a sliding-headstock Swiss lathe guides the bar in a bushing right at the cut, holding straightness and size far better than a conventional lathe. What matters when machining liquid cooling cold plates and connectors?

Two things decide whether a liquid cooling part actually works: the flatness of the sealing face, which keeps the thermal interface layer thin and even, and the internal flow channel, which must be free of burrs where cross-drillings intersect. We hold ±0.005 mm on cold plate sealing faces as standard production tolerance. Details: CNC liquid cooling connectors.
